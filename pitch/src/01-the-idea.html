The problem

Numbers arrive after the facts. Stores see the facts first.

A luxury brand runs hundreds of stores in dozens of countries. Every day headquarters decides what to replenish, where to move stock and what to tell customers.

The tools behind those decisions are good, but slow by design:

  • The forecast learns from history: last season, last year.
  • Certified sales arrive the next day, after the facts.
  • Demand that never became a sale doesn’t arrive at all. If size M is sold out, the till records nothing.

Meanwhile, every evening each store writes a closing email in its own language. There are hundreds a day, written in a hurry, and almost nobody reads them against the numbers.

Why it matters

Three things the numbers can’t see

The store notes are often the only place where these facts exist. Ignore them and the system draws the wrong conclusion from the right numbers.

1 · Invisible demand

The sale that didn’t happen

A size runs out. The till records zero. The forecast learns “low demand” and the next allocation gets smaller. The customer who asked and left is only in the store manager’s email.

2 · The reason why

Same sales, different stories

Two products each sell five pieces. For one, customers try it and the fit is wrong. For the other, they ask for size M and there is none. The numbers are identical, but one needs a product decision and the other needs stock.

3 · Local events

Low sales ≠ low demand

Scaffolding in front of the door, a broken window, a till outage. Sales drop, but nobody wants the product less. Pulling stock away from that store would be the wrong move.

And the notes are noisy. The same message is sent twice and forwarded. Figures are corrected the next morning (“38k, not 83k”). Product names are wrong, staff exaggerate, five people describe the same customer. Reading them is not enough: they must be checked against stock, sales and each other.

The idea

Listen to the stores, keep the receipts, hand over a short list of decisions.

Listen

Read what stores already write

Closing emails, voice notes, reports and chats, as they are, in any of 10+ languages. Each note is broken into single facts, and every fact keeps the exact sentence it came from.

Connect

Tie every fact to the business

“The black one” becomes a product, a size, a store and a date. Copies and corrections are merged. Each fact is set against stock, sales, the forecast and the context: weather, events, tourism.

Decide

A queue of cases, not a feed

Related facts become a case with an owner and a deadline. An investigator gathers the missing checks and proposes feasible options. A person approves.

AI reads. Code counts. Humans decide.
The rule that makes it trustworthy

Each side does only what it is good at

AI reads
  • Understands hurried, multilingual text.
  • Picks which product a note means, or says it isn’t sure.
  • Decides which check to run next.
  • Never produces a number that ends up in a calculation.
Code counts
  • Quantities, stock run-out, transfer feasibility, forecast scenarios.
  • Same inputs, same answer, every time.
  • Every number traceable to a row or a quote.
Humans decide
  • Nothing executes by itself: approving creates a draft.
  • “Hold” and “verify first” are always on the table.
  • Long-term choices (production, planned scarcity) get an insight, never a push.

Why a business person should care

Planners can audit it: every line on a card links back to the sentence a store wrote. No black box moves stock, and when evidence is missing the system says “verify”. It does not guess.

One story, start to finish

The cardigan in size M

Thu 8 Oct · Milan closing email
“il cardigan logo M è finito, ce lo chiedono in continuazione” (the logo cardigan in M is finished, they keep asking for it)
Read
Two facts: a stockout (exactly zero) and unmet demand. “Keep asking” stays vague: no invented number.
Connect
Linked to the logo cardigan, size M, Milan store. The runner-up candidate stays visible.
Check
The second Milan store writes “only one cardigan M left”, but the system shows 3. Tessera flags the mismatch as its own card and does not quietly pick one. The regional warehouse has none until the end of October.
Investigate
The investigator reads the evidence, looks up stock across stores, checks the delivery route and scores the options.
Decide
Allocation receives a card: hold, verify the stock, ask the store, or transfer. Each option shows which checks pass or fail. A person chooses.
What sales would have said

The next day’s certified sales show zero cardigans in M: it looks like nobody wants it. Without the note, the forecast learns the wrong lesson.

Honest ending

In the recorded run the delivery route was unknown, so the system recommended notifying the owner rather than moving stock. That is a valid outcome: it won’t propose a move it can’t check.

Boundaries

What Tessera is, and what it isn’t

It is
  • A layer on top of the forecast, ERP and logistics already in place
  • A queue of decisions, each with an owner, a deadline and evidence for and against
  • A memory of evidence: every claim keeps the sentence it came from
  • Multilingual by default: Italian, French, German, Spanish, English, Japanese, Korean, Chinese…
  • Modular: turn a source or a step on or off and rerun
It isn’t
  • A new forecast model. Their forecast stays as it is; we annotate it.
  • A replacement for certified reporting. Store signals stay labelled as uncertified.
  • An autopilot that moves stock. It proposes; people approve.
  • A chatbot that summarises emails. Summaries lose the evidence; we keep it.

Shared vocabulary

The words we use, and what they mean

ObservationOne fact from one note: “size M sold out at Milan since Thursday”.
EvidenceThe exact sentence behind an observation. No quote, no fact.
LinkConnecting “the black one” to a product, colour and size.
AbstentionThe system declines to guess. It is a feature: a wrong confident link is worse than none.
Origin vs documentTen forwarded copies are ten documents but one origin: one confirmation, not ten.
CaseFacts about the same store, product and issue, grouped, with an owner, a priority and a deadline.
VerifyA case status: something must be checked before anyone acts.
BaselineThe brand’s existing forecast. We never overwrite it.
ScenarioLow, central and high outlooks shown beside the baseline, only when the evidence allows.
InvestigatorThe AI agent that decides which checks to run next. It can read, never approve.
Option + checksHold, verify, ask, replenish or transfer, each with pass, fail or unknown checks.
Dev / hold-outStores we tuned on vs stores kept aside for honest final scores.
