The loop

When the agent asks a store “which sweatshirt, the hoodie or the crewneck?”, the reply comes back in as a new source and the case updates.

The clock

Every fact records when we could have known it. Replaying “Thursday evening” uses only what existed on Thursday evening: no hindsight.

The thread

From the case card you can click back to the exact sentence the store wrote. This is what makes the output trustworthy.

Stations 1 and 2

Collect what exists, read it into facts

1 · Collect

What happens. We take the sources as they are: the stores’ closing emails (2,056 in the test world, in 10+ languages), optional guided voice notes, conflicting reports from controlling and regions, the ERP (catalogue, sales, stock, forecast) and outside context such as weather, holidays, events and tourism.

Why the business cares. No new system for the stores. They keep writing the email they already write.

Guard. Each source gets a “known since” time. Sales are certified the next day, and a report counts from the day it was issued, not the day its period ended.

“We read what the stores already write.”

2 · Read

What happens. One email often holds several facts of different kinds: a stockout, a customer request, a fit complaint, a broken counter. The AI splits them and tags each one, and every fact must come with the exact sentence that supports it.

Why the business cares. Any claim can be checked in one click. Vague stays vague: “lots of customers” is never turned into “7”.

Guard. Code checks that the quote really appears, word for word. If it doesn’t, the fact is rejected and logged, never invented.

“No quote, no fact.”

Stations 3 and 4

Know what they mean, and don’t count the same thing twice

3 · Link

What happens. Store staff never write product codes. They write “la Aurea nera”, “粉紅 Aria” or “the long black one”. We shortlist plausible products (what the store carries first, then the whole catalogue) and the AI chooses among them. If no candidate is clear, it abstains and the case goes to “verify”.

Why the business cares. It is the hardest and most valuable step. A wrong confident link moves the wrong stock.

Proof so far. On the dev sample: 43 links accepted, 0 wrong; 21% abstained instead of guessing.

“When it isn’t sure, it asks.”

4 · Clean

What happens. The same mail is sent twice, forwarded to a regional manager or corrected the next morning (“38k, not 83k”). Five staff may describe the same customer. Tesseract keeps every document but counts origins: copies merge, and a correction replaces the old fact only from its own timestamp.

Why the business cares. Rumours don’t snowball into false urgency, and the history is never rewritten.

Guard. Uncertain overlaps stay separate and their quantities are not added. Three reports ≠ three customers.

“Repeating a rumour doesn’t make it truer.”

Station 5

Cases: one queue, each item with an owner and a deadline

What happens. Facts about the same store, product and issue become one case. Each case shows what changed, the evidence for and against, how many independent origins support it, what is still unknown, who owns it and by when a decision is needed.

Context, not trigger. Weather or an event can make a case more urgent, but it never creates demand on its own. Scaffolding in front of the store produces a retail-ops card and the forecast is frozen, not cut: low sales are not low demand.

Silence is not a resolution. If a store stops writing, the case doesn’t close. An old stock figure doesn’t cancel a new note.

“Same sales, different reasons, different decisions.”

Owners are a configuration file: another business can remap them without new code.

Station 6

The investigator does the legwork, and stops at the button

What happens. For a case, an AI agent decides what to check next, as an analyst would at 9 am: read the evidence, look up stock across nearby stores and the warehouse, check the delivery route, score the options, or draft a question to the store in its own language. It works within a fixed budget of steps.

What it cannot do. Approve, book, change a number, or override a failed check. Its tools are read-only. Text inside emails is treated as data, never as instructions.

“The agent investigates. It can’t press the button.”

Recorded path A · cardigan M, Milan

evidence → size M stock, both colours, in 3 stores → route from the second Milan store → score options → notify owner

Recorded path B · “a sweatshirt”, New York

evidence → ambiguous product (hoodie or crewneck?) → draft a clarification in English → verify

Why it matters: different evidence led to different steps, so the agent is actually choosing what to do. A fixed script would take the same path every time.

And the forecast? The brand’s forecast stays the baseline, untouched. When the evidence is strong enough, Tesseract shows low, central and high scenarios beside it, each with its assumption and expiry. Otherwise it gives the reason it abstained.

Station 7

Options, not orders

The menu
  • hold always available
  • verify stock always available
  • ask the store
  • replenish from warehouse
  • transfer between stores
  • notify the owner
Every move must pass every check
  • The donor store won’t run short itself, and keeps its safety stock
  • The units are sellable, not reserved or on quality hold
  • Stock arrives before the store runs out, not after
  • The route exists and doesn’t cross a customs border it can’t
  • The size is right: a colour total doesn’t prove size M is there

If any required check is unknown, the move is blocked and “verify” is offered instead.

Approval

A person approves or rejects. Approval creates a draft tied to the exact data it was based on; if the data changed, the old proposal is refused. Clicking twice can’t send it twice.

Reports per office

The same facts feed a short brief for allocation, retail ops, product and marketing. Every statement links back to its source.
