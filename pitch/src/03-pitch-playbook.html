What this means for how we talk

  • 40% is “does it really work end to end?” It is also the tie-breaker. Show one path working, from the email to the decision card, rather than ten half-built features.
  • 20% is agents. Show the investigator choosing its checks, not just summarising.
  • The rest is believability. Name a buyer, a pain and a way in. Show that it runs on Fastweb’s models.

What the brand told us

Forecast and transaction systems are slow by design and won’t change. Stores feel the market first. A stockout costs brand image, not just a sale. Decide as early as possible, and humans decide. Short term means moving stock; long term means production, which is far too high-stakes for AI alone.

Three messages. Repeat them.

If the jury remembers only three sentences

1

“Stores see it first.”

Value: we catch what sales show a day later, and what sales never show: the customer who asked for a size that wasn’t there.

2

“Every claim has a receipt.”

Trust: every line on a card links back to the sentence a store wrote. When the evidence is thin, the system says “verify”, not “buy”.

3

“Options, not orders.”

Adoption: nothing moves without a person. That lowers the risk of trying it: a planner gets an analyst, not a replacement.

Opening line

“Tonight is Thursday. Today’s sales don’t exist yet. And the sale that didn’t happen, because size M ran out, will never exist. But at eight o’clock the store manager wrote it down. We read it.”

The business case

Where the money leaks today

Sales you never see

A size runs out and the till records zero. The forecast learns “low demand”, the next allocation shrinks, and the gap feeds itself.

A client who leaves empty-handed

In luxury a stockout also costs brand image: the client who came for a piece and didn’t find it. The brand said so directly.

Stock in the wrong place

Markdowns are rare in luxury, so surplus is expensive too. One store waits for a size while another sits on it.

Moves based on rumours

A forwarded email counted three times, or stock pulled from a store whose “low sales” were really scaffolding at the door.

Hours nobody has

Hundreds of emails a day in 10+ languages. Nobody reads them against stock and sales, so the knowledge stays in inboxes.

Decisions that come too late

Logistics is slow. Every day of warning before the shipping cut-off is what makes an option still possible.

Don’t quote a euro figure. We have no real data, and an invented ROI loses credibility. Offer the frame instead: value = sizes recovered × full-price margin + wrong moves avoided × cost of a move + analyst hours returned. Then: “a pilot would measure it on your numbers.”

Some events are seen only by Tessera. A quality defect reported by all 12 dev stores, mostly on day one. Requests for a colour the store doesn’t carry. A launch below expectations. No sales or stock rule ever flags these.

Say it with the caveat, every time

“Synthetic data built for the challenge, with decoys and noise; measured on the stores we developed on. The kept-aside stores are scored once, at the end.” Until that run is done, don’t present any hold-out number.

Application potential

Who buys it, how it gets in, why now

Buyer

HQ allocation and merchandising.

Retail ops, product/quality and marketing get their own view of the same facts. First context: a network of directly operated boutiques. Franchise and multi-brand come later.

Proposed pilot

One region, read-only, the closing emails stores already send. Planners keep their tools and review the queue each morning. We measure decisions taken earlier, cases planners judged useful, and false alarms.

Why now

AI can finally read hurried multilingual text reliably. Vendors are opening their ERPs to agents through a standard connection (MCP), and Tessera already exposes one, read-only. It runs on Fastweb’s AI models.

Closing line
Your stores already know. Tessera makes sure headquarters hears them, with the receipt attached.
